MAKING SECURELY – SCRIPT
========================

Tone: Casual, straightforward, slightly cinematic. Not fast-paced.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[0:00–2:10] OPENING – PERSONAL HOOK, BUILD CURIOSITY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[Wide shot – Dark room, only streetlight + monitor glow]
[Ambient sound – distant cars, soft keyboard clicks]
Voiceover (calm, reflective):
“Eighteen years of my life went by so quick.
And when I look back… I already see myself in a completely different world.
Unique campus… new faces… new environment…"

[Cut to – Alarm clock: 3:00 AM]
[Close up – Hand slaps alarm, feet hit the floor]
Voiceover:
“Every day… 3 AM.
I’d roll off my bed and do push-ups - just to wake my body up.
Then I’d take a good shower… make a good breakfast…
because I wanted my mornings to feel solid.
Like no matter what happened later… at least I started strong.”

[Cut to – Coffee brewing / eggs cooking / backpack getting packed]
[Close up – Keys in hand]
Voiceover:
“By 5 AM I was on the road.”

[Driving shot – highway, sunrise starting]
Voiceover:
“An hour later… 6 AM… I’m in the parking lot.”

[Inside car – notebook open, highlighter, quick review]
[Close up – Notes flipping]
Voiceover:
“I’d study in my car for a little bit.
Just a quick review… because at 7:30 my first class started.”

[Montage – Campus walk, shoes on pavement, classroom door opening]
Voiceover:
“And it was five classes back-to-back.
So I’d finish around 1 PM… and I still wasn’t done.”

[Cut to – After school: laptop open, club flyer, competition checklist]
Voiceover:
“Homework. Clubs. Competitions.
Literally after school.”

[Close up – You exhale, stare at the screen]
You (in frame, honest):
“That was my first semester of college.
And I’m telling you that because… that’s the environment this project was born in.”

[Cut to – CS50 folder on screen, scrolling past weeks]
You:
“On top of all that, I was enrolled in CS50 — Harvard’s course.
And I’m not even exaggerating when I say… I was like 90% done with the problem sets…
and I still didn’t know what my final project was.”

[Pause – let it sit]

[Close up – You lean in a little]
You:
“Then my school announced this competition called the AI Contest.
And that kinda sparked interest.”

[Cut to – Screen recording: contest prompt / notes page]
You:
“I saw the prompt and it was basically like…
you have to make a program or a system that has AI integrated into it.
And the point is to simulate coming up with an idea, making it exist,
and actually selling it to companies.”

[Close up – You, half laugh]
You:
“So now I’m stressed, right?
CS50 final project is still sitting there… and now this contest is sitting there too…
and I’m like, okay — I need one idea that can do both.”

[Close up – Pen tapping / typing in notes]
You:
“And that’s where the ‘one big project’ idea came from.”

[Close up – Terminal / editor, fast little montage but not too fast]
Voiceover:
“That decision started a chain reaction: late nights, a lot of learning curves,
and way more time than I’m comfortable admitting.”

[Close up – You, half-smile]
You:
“I’m not gonna name it yet.
But if you’ve ever clicked something questionable on autopilot and immediately regretted it…
this whole project is basically my response to that feeling.”

[Cut to black – 1 second]

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[2:10–3:05] REVEAL – SECURELY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[Cut to – App open on screen, clean shot]
You:
“Alright. The program is called Securely.”

[Screen recording – Slow pan over the Securely UI, not rushed]
Voiceover:
“Securely is an AI–powered security assistant that quietly watches your screen in the background.”

[Close up – You in frame, medium shot]
You:
“Think of it like an overprotective linter… but for real life.
Phishing emails, sketchy pop–ups, weird websites — the stuff you click when you’re tired and your brain is running on low power mode.”

[Screen recording – show the flow visually: screenshot → OCR → AI → alert]
Voiceover:
“It takes screenshots, uses OCR to read what’s there, and sends that text to an AI model to check for security threats.”

[Close up – Code on screen, quick highlight]
Voiceover:
“In the CS50 write-up I literally had it listed like this: screenshots every second, OCR with `pytesseract`, screen capture with `mss`,
and then the AI analysis with Gemini — all wrapped in a desktop app.”

[Close up – You]
You:
“It’s meant to be that extra pair of eyes that quietly asks,
‘Hey… are you sure you want to click that?’”


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[3:05–4:45] HOW IT STARTED – CS50 & THE AI CONTEST
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[Walking into frame – You walk into your room / workspace, drop your backpack on a chair]
You:
“So, how did this all start?”

[Sit down at desk, lean back a little]
You:
“I was enrolled in CS50… and I was, like, 90% done with the course. I had gone through all the problem sets, all the lectures… 
but I still had no idea what my final project was going to be.”

[Cut to – B–roll of CS50 materials: course page on your screen, maybe the CS50 folder in your files]
Voiceover:
“I had this deadline staring at me. Final project due, and I had nothing I was truly excited about. 
At the same time, my school announced something called the AI Contest.”

[Close up – You talking directly to camera]
You:
“The contest sounded… intense. You had to build a program or system that integrated AI. 
But it wasn’t just ‘make something cool.’ The whole idea was to pretend this was a real product — 
something you’d pitch to companies, something you’d actually sell.”

[Cut to – Over–the–shoulder shot of you scrolling through competition details on screen]
Voiceover:
“That’s when something clicked. I thought… what if I could build *one* project that checked both boxes? 
My CS50 final… and a serious submission for this AI Contest.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[3:45–5:15] FINDING A PARTNER – THE BUSINESS SIDE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[Close up – You, relaxed, maybe slightly smiling]
You:
“The first thing I realized: I’m not a business major. I can code… but sales, marketing, business strategy? That’s a whole different world.”

[Cut to – Empty chair beside you]
You:
“So I knew I needed a partner. 
Someone who could actually think about this like a product, not just a cool script running in the background.”

[Cut to – B–roll: maybe a notebook with ‘Securely’ written, arrows, and ‘Market’, ‘Branding’, ‘Pitch’ scribbled]
Voiceover:
“That’s when I met this really cool girl. She’s a business major, and honestly… she’s amazing.”

[Close up – You looking genuinely appreciative]
You:
“She helped me so much throughout this project. She didn’t just say, ‘Oh yeah, that’s cool.’ 
She gave real feedback. Honest opinions. We’d go back and forth on features, what actually matters to users, 
how to explain Securely to people who don’t speak ‘tech.’ 
That’s when Securely stopped being just a coding assignment and started feeling like… a real product.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[5:15–7:00] OFFICIALLY STARTING SECURELY – CS50 TIME CRUNCH
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[Draw on the whiteboard – Simple architecture diagram: “Screen” → “Screenshot” → “OCR” → “AI” → “Alert”]
You (while drawing):
“Okay, so this is kind of how Securely works behind the scenes. 
You’ve got your screen, we grab an image, convert it to text with OCR, send that text to an AI model, 
and then we decide: Is this safe… or is this sketchy?”

[Cut to – Close up of the whiteboard finished drawing]
Voiceover:
“Once I had the idea, the clock started ticking. 
CS50’s final project wasn’t going to wait for me to feel ‘ready’.”

[Sit back at desk, slightly stressed but smiling]
You:
“For CS50, I was on a serious time crunch. I had about one month left to create something *big*. 
Something I’d be proud to submit to Harvard’s CS50, and also something good enough for the AI Contest.”

[Close up – You rubbing your eyes, a coffee or energy drink on the desk]
You:
“Every day… more pressure. I was still a full–time student. 
I had five classes, I was involved in clubs, competitions, this Harvard course, and on top of all that, 
I was trying to build side projects to make my resume actually mean something.”

[Slow montage – Shots of you walking through campus, typing on a laptop in different places, late–night scenes]
Voiceover:
“So yeah… life was kind of insane. But that’s also what made finishing Securely feel so important.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[7:00–9:00] THE GRIND – 200+ HOURS, ALL–NIGHTERS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[Close up – Monitor showing lines of Securely code (no need to read it, just aesthetic)]
Voiceover:
“I lost count at some point, but I know I put in over 200 hours into this project. 
Debugging. Refactoring. Rewriting logic that I thought was good… until it broke everything.”

[Cut to – Terminal showing tests/logs scrolling]
You (quick, understated):
“You know it’s serious when you start naming bugs like they’re roommates.”

[Over–the–shoulder shot – You scrolling through the CS50 project folder]
Voiceover:
“Most of those hours weren’t glamorous. It was just me, in front of a screen, trying to fix one tiny bug… 
that no one will ever see… but that makes the whole thing actually work.”

[Close up – Empty cans / cups: Monster, Starbucks, etc.]
You (light laugh):
“I’m not proud of how much caffeine went into this project… 
but let’s just say that Monster and Starbucks were definitely co–founders of Securely.”

[Slow shot – You staring at the screen, visibly tired]
Voiceover:
“There were days where I questioned if it was even worth it. 
Like, why am I doing this to myself? Why am I this stressed… over a project?”

[Close up – You, thoughtful]
You:
“But every time I got something working — like the OCR pipeline, or the AI threat detection, or the UI finally looking clean — 
it reminded me why I love building things. Because you watch your ideas turn into something real.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[9:00–11:00] LEARNING CURVES – TECHNICAL CHALLENGES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[Draw on the whiteboard – New section: “Learning Curve” with bullet points: OCR, API, Threads, UX]
You:
“Securely forced me to level up in a lot of ways.”

[Close up – You pointing to “OCR” on the board]
You:
“First: OCR. Getting the computer to actually *read* the screen isn’t as simple as just ‘take a screenshot.’ 
I had to figure out how to capture efficiently, convert that to text, and then clean that text up so the AI actually understood it.”

[Cut to – Screen recording of code: OCR function, AI request, etc. Scroll slowly.]
Voiceover:
“Then there was AI. Handling API calls, rate limits, timeouts, errors — 
you don’t think about that when you first imagine the idea. But in reality, it’s a huge part of making the app feel reliable.”

[Close up – You shrugging]
You:
“It was a lot of ‘it works on my machine’… and then my machine immediately proving me wrong.”

[Close up – “Threads” on whiteboard]
You:
“Multi–threading was another big one. Securely needs to monitor your screen without freezing the whole app. 
That meant learning how to run work in the background while keeping the interface smooth.”

[Cut to – You at desk, interacting with the app calmly]
Voiceover:
“I also had to care about user experience. Not just, ‘Does it run?’ but ‘Would *I* actually want this running on my machine every day?’ 
That changed a lot of tiny decisions — how notifications look, how aggressive the alerts are, how easy it is to pause monitoring.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[11:00–13:00] PROS & CONS – BEING HONEST ABOUT SECURELY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[Medium shot – You seated, more relaxed now]
You:
“I want to be honest about Securely. It’s not perfect. It’s not some magical AI that can stop every single threat on the internet.”

[Close up – App UI again, maybe showing a log with some detections]
You:
“Let me start with the pros.”

[List on screen – Simple text slide: “Pros”]
Voiceover:
“**Pros:**  
– It actually works. It can catch phishing–looking content, sketchy messages, and suspicious wording on your screen.  
– It runs in the background and doesn’t need you to constantly babysit it.  
– It’s practical. This isn’t just a school demo — it’s solving a real problem people face every day.”

[List on screen – Simple text slide: “Cons”]
Voiceover:
“**Cons:**  
– It can be a little too aggressive sometimes, flagging things that are technically safe but *look* suspicious.  
– It’s not lightweight in the sense of ‘oh yeah, it’s just a tiny script.’ It uses OCR and AI, so it has a cost in performance and API usage.  
– It’s still evolving. There are edge cases it doesn’t handle perfectly yet.”

[Cut back to you]
You:
“But honestly… I’m okay with that. I’d rather it be a bit over–protective than miss something important.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[17:30–19:30] WHY SECURELY MATTERS TO ME
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[Close up – You at your desk, lights a bit warmer, calmer mood]

“Because it’s personal for me in a way that I don’t think I ever really said out loud.

“So when I built Securely, I wasn’t trying to make something that only tech people understand.

[Close up – You, a bit more vulnerable]
You:
“It’s also a reminder that I’m not doing this alone. 
From my CS50 instructors, to my classmates, to the AI Contest, to my business partner, to friends like Kayla…
